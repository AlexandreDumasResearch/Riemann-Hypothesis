# 06 — Local saturation replaces the universal crystalline length problem
Alexandre Dumas · ORCID 0009-0003-1695-0086 · 27 November 2026
New conditional reduction, after C8/C9. Not a proof of the global bound.

## 1. A fixed local augmentation lattice
For each w|ℓ let Γ=G_{E_w}, H_w=span_O u(Γ) and I_w=span_O(u(g)−1:g∈Γ), inside the actual complete family of integral matrices. Give H_w and I_w the left action u(g). They are finite free O-modules, and H_w/I_w is cyclic with trivial action. Put c_w(g)=u(g)−1. It is a cocycle in I_w, a coboundary in H_w.

**Qualitative input Q:** the actual local u_x are potentially crystalline (the pinned source establishes their adapted class). Then H_w and I_w are potentially crystalline as subrepresentations of the direct sum of columns of u_x. Over a finite extension making H_w,I_w crystalline, the rational crystalline period functor is exact on these representations. In particular the image of 1 in H_w/I_w has an invariant period lift in B_cris⊗H_w. Averaging this lift over the finitely many cosets of a normal such extension makes it invariant under Γ (characteristic zero). The difference of 1 and the lift belongs to B_cris⊗I_w and has coboundary c_w. If H_w/I_w is torsion, its rationalization is zero, so the same argument uses lift zero. Therefore c_w is crystalline. This argument uses standard qualitative exactness of the crystalline period functor and the compatibility of B_cris with the source Bmax. **These precise source interfaces need a formal check**; it is not an elementary proof of p-adic Hodge theory. Alternatively an exact local period lift is a sufficient explicit hypothesis. Compare Basic.lean, crysCocycles definition, and TateDualBound.lean, comesFromClass_of_mem_ribetCocycles, which uses graph lattices depending on truncation.

## 2. Integral equivariance relations and their saturation
Define
$$F_w=L^*\otimes_O I_w,\quad R_w=\langle(\varphi h)\otimes i-\varphi\otimes hi: h\in H_w,i\in I_w,\varphi\in L^*\rangle_O,\quad P_w=F_w/R_w.$$
Thus P_w=L*⊗_{H_w}I_w. Since all modules H_w,I_w,L* have finite O-bases, the relation module R_w is generated by finitely many explicit rows using their basis elements. No infinite Galois cohomology system appears in this matrix. Hom_O(P_w,O) corresponds to H_w-equivariant integral maps I_w→L. By Q, composition with c_w gives an integral crystalline cocycle of L (functoriality of becoming a coboundary).

For any actual crystalline relation row j, form its vector j_w=Σφ_i⊗(u(g_i)−1)∈F_w. The defining relation holds for every integral equivariant map. Therefore j_w annihilates every element of Hom_O(P_w,O). Over a DVR the intersection of the kernels of all these forms is exactly the torsion of P_w: decompose P_w into free and torsion parts. Consequently,
$$\boxed{j_w\in R_w^{\rm sat}:=F_w\cap (R_w\otimes_O\operatorname{Frac}O).}\tag{C10a}$$
The proof uses Q only to certify that integral equivariant maps give crystalline cocycles. It does not use the universal quantitative dualDefect theorem. The torsion R_w^sat/R_w is computed by Smith normal form of the finite equivariance relation matrix, discarding free directions. This also treats χ=1; no scalar separation from the trivial character is assumed.

## 3. Relative image after actual ties
For a main summand let K,T,π_K be the source-normalized modules of C8. Natural map F_w→P_ρ followed by π_K kills R_w, and define S=K/T. For a character take S=P_χ/E_δ as in C9c. The natural map ψ_w:F_w→S kills R_w as well. Both maps preserve the relevant crystalline relation images. Let W be sum of ψ_w(R_w^sat) in S. By C10a, images of all crystalline relations lie in W. Thus at finite truncation q=ℓ^M,
$$\boxed{D_{\rho,M}^{\rm tied}\le\operatorname{len}\frac{W+qS}{qS}\le\operatorname{len} W\le\sum_{w|\ell}\operatorname{len}(R_w^{\rm sat}/R_w).}\tag{C10★}$$
For characters, same statement bounds C9c with actual truncation ν. W is finite torsion because each R_w^sat/R_w is finite torsion. This is a quantitative *sufficient* replacement, potentially weaker or stronger than the universal p-adic Hodge bound; do not claim an improvement without estimating the actual matrices. It also avoids paying for global Tor or global trace discriminants.

For sharper relative calculation put F=⊕F_w, R=⊕R_w, ψ=Σψ_w, Z=ker ψ. Then
$$\boxed{W\simeq R^{\rm sat}/(R^{\rm sat}\cap Z),\qquad \operatorname{len} W=\operatorname{len}(R^{\rm sat}/R)-\operatorname{len}((R^{\rm sat}\cap Z)/R).}\tag{C10b}$$
The favorable overlap measures precisely those local equivariance obstructions killed by global trace relations and ties. Both sides are finite. A scalar c with c R^sat⊂Z annihilates W. If W needs s generators, len W≤s v_O(c). Use actual invariant factors rather than maximum times rank whenever possible. Rescale v_O to vℓ(ℓ)=1 before inserting into C9.

## 4. Stress tests
1. Integral equivariance over the residue field does not imply integral lifting: matrix [ℓ^h] has rational kernel zero and mod ℓ^M kernel length min(h,M). Saturation defect can be arbitrarily large, even with one equation.
2. Rational semisimplicity of the global family does not imply local semisimplicity or integral saturation. No assumed rational decomposition of local Galois representations enters the proof above.
3. For an abstract relation matrix diag(1,ℓ^h,0) the torsion length is h, while a free direction remains. Counting all of P_w would grow with M; counting saturation ignores the free direction correctly.
4. If ties kill the ℓ^h class, W=0 even though local saturation has length h. This illustrates the exact potential gain, not a numerical computation on actual Galois representations.
5. The method only uses qualitative crystalline admissibility; do not infer that quantitative p-adic Hodge theory can be avoided unless the new global Smith-length bound is established. In particular, Hodge weights alone do not bound arbitrary integral congruences.
6. For rank-one χ, relation module corresponds to mχ I_w under O⊗I_w≅I_w, where mχ=ker(H_w→O). Its saturation quotient is the torsion of I_w/mχ I_w. For trivial χ this is I_w/I_w². A principal augmentation ideal with a unit separator gives zero; neither is guaranteed on actual local data.

## 5. New concrete proof obligation
Construct a uniform bound on relative Smith lengths
$$\boxed{\sum_{\ell,j,\rho} w_{\ell,j,\rho}\log\ell\,\operatorname{normLength}(W_{\ell,j,\rho})\ll fa\log b.}\tag{C11}$$
Weights must be the actual determinant multiplicities; main summand uniformity in M is automatic in C10 if the fixed data and Q hold. Tate characters retain their actual ν, or use the same stronger uniform bound. Need explicit local representation matrices or integral polynomial relations implying a sufficiently small conductor for the relative saturation image. The source's actual u_x are not supplied as numerical matrices, so this is still a substantive mathematical obligation. The original crystalline theorem remains fallback.

Next priority: use actual local inertia polynomials and weight/period constraints to show the relative saturation image is small. Avoid full discriminants and arbitrary p-adic matrix examples as proof of the uniform estimate. Formalize the elementary image and annihilator step first; the qualitative period argument and the global estimate are separate.
