## Exact difference of determinantal divisors (C12)
Continue C10 with F=⊕F_w finite free over the coefficient DVR O, R=⊕R_w the relation lattice, ψ:F→S the actual tied or tame quotient map, Z=ker ψ, R⊂Z⊂F. Set r=rank R. Both R and Z are free. Let A be the matrix of a finite generating family for R in an O-basis of F, B the matrix of an O-basis of Z, and C the coordinate matrix of the same generating family for R in Z, so A=BC. For a rank-r matrix D let d_r(D) be the ideal generated by its r×r minors, with d_0=O.

The actual reachable saturation image W=ψ(R^sat) is finite, and
$$\boxed{\operatorname{length}_O W=v_O(d_r(A))-v_O(d_r(C)).}\tag{C12★}$$
Proof: Smith normal form identifies len tors(F/R)=v(d_r(A)) and len tors(Z/R)=v(d_r(C)). The kernel of tors(F/R)→F/Z is tors(Z/R); both are finite torsion modules. Therefore exact additivity gives the displayed difference. Equivalently R^sat/(R^sat∩Z)≅W. Cauchy–Binet gives d_r(A)⊂d_r(C), hence nonnegative difference. This also covers r=0.

At every coefficient place use the source-normalized vℓ (divide uniformizer valuation by ramification index). If qualitative period input Q of C10 holds, the weighted crystalline budget can be bounded by
$$\boxed{\mathfrak A_{\rm crys}\le\sum_{\ell,j,\rho}w_{\ell,j,\rho}\log\ell\,[v_\ell(d_r(A_{\ell,j,\rho}))-v_\ell(d_r(C_{\ell,j,\rho}))].}\tag{C13}$$
Here the determinants are of the actual relation modules, not Hecke eigenvalues or periods. Uniformity over the admissible coefficient embeddings and actual weights must be retained. No claim that the global sum has been bounded. The favorable second term must not be dropped prematurely. This is the same signed lattice-index principle as D2 on a new exact arithmetic module.

## Relative prime support and local vanishing
If the inclusion Z↪F is saturated (F/Z torsion-free), then R^sat⊂Z and W=0, even if R itself is not saturated. More generally a nonzero scalar c with c tors(F/Z)=0 kills W; W is a submodule of tors(F/Z). The following equivalent condition avoids constructing any central separator:
$$W=0\iff R^{sat}\subseteq Z\iff v(d_r(A))=v(d_r(C)).$$
For r>0 the prime support of W is contained in the prime support of d_r(A); it can be strictly smaller. One unit maximal-rank minor of A suffices for W=0. If a presentation of the actual tied quotient F/Z has a known small torsion conductor, use it instead of the full trace discriminant. In the main block, Z is defined by the actual tied quotient from C8, not an arbitrary submodule that may be enlarged.

## What is genuinely new here
The determinant ratio is an elementary consequence of Smith normal form and exactness, with classical antecedents. It furnishes an explicit finite algebraic objective for the source's relative crystalline cost, conditional on C10. To eliminate the quantitative Hodge supplier, one must now bound the **difference**, not independently bound both determinant heights. Neither the existence of finite matrices nor a formal proof of this identity establishes that uniform bound.
